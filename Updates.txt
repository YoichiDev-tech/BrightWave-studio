05/08/2026 - Documenting future improvements (added or not)

This file will contain and breakdown all new features from this point onwards.

As the job has been pretty successful so far (the coding part at least),
I decided to breakdown every piece of implementation, why to add and why to not.
This way, I'll leave my future self with a good review and also various checkpoints
where I can decide to focus on and speak more about it into details or not.

With that said, my first feature at this point of the app, is to work on the testimonials.
I don't want to have a situation where my user is using the platform and I ask for a review,
and there is not a "built-in" system that allows this on the same platform without needed to
change app: basically, I'm trying to build an all-in-one platform so the user can have on it
what they need as long as it follows the platform's ideology.

Testimonials 
I do not need testimonials right now, more likely a 'testimonials engine', something that
could actually: 
- collect feedback automatically
- stores it cleanly
- lets me "deal and cure" what appears publicly
- fits the strategy
- does not make the landing page too long or cluttered


Options I have right now based on the need and plain thinking
- A home page mini section + full testimonials page
- Only a full testimonials page 
- Only a small home page section

Best approach idea idea I got so far, is to "build" a hybrid structure, meaning
- a small testimonial preview (3 as it is the most common number)
placed right before the contact section
- a full testimonials page that holds all reviews, filters, categories and some future
expansion possibilities

* Possible reason why this is or could be the best approach
- the home page remains fast, focused and conversion-oriented
so adding a small preview in the home page it leaves it clean and adds a punchy text
- a dedicated page that can be scaled with 'no limits'
meaning, to put this simply into numbers, having 5 - 15 - 60 testimonials or 
maybe also video testimonials / or niche-specific testimonials, must need a their own space
and cannot be added to the home page
* Only a full testimonial page might be bad/week
- no social proof on the home page
- visitors may never check testimonials link
* Only on the home page section might also be bad/week
- home page becomes too long
- cannot scale over 3/4 testimonials
- probably lose SEO and also lose ability to send prospects a dedicated testimonials link
* No testimonials until later might also be bad/week
- possibility to lose opportunities
- delay building social proof
- lose the ability to store review especially when someone wants to leave it
- and looking unprepared and at an amateur level


Architect the testimonial system
- define the testimonial data model
using the possible structure below
{
  id: string;
  name?: string;
  company?: string;
  rating: number; // 1–5
  message: string;
  intent: "audit" | "build"; // aligns with the funnel
  createdAt: Date;
  approved: boolean; // decide what goes public
}
- plan the capture mechanism (planning)
capture triggers
# after a successful contact form submission
# after a project delivery
# after a user interacts with the site for a while
# after clicking 'Was it helpful?' or any section
- capture form fields
# rating (required)
# message (required)
# name (optional)
# company (required / optional, to be chosen)
# permission toggle 'Can this be displayed publicly?'

this way I can collect both the testimonials and the product feedback

- plan the storage layer
supabase (already used, fast and simple-ish)


Starting point - Design the home page testimonial preview 

- section placement = above the contact section
- section title = "Trusted by founders, creators and small businesses"

Layout (card layout)
- audit testimonial = someone who has an improved existing site mentioned clarity, speed and modern design
- build testimonial = someone who had an idea and BrightWave-studio built it mentioned smooth process and custom design
- general praise = fast communication, professionalism, reliability
- CTA under the section = a small link saying "See all testimonials =>"

23/08/2026 - Fixing the chat bug, and starting the "second brain" of the operation

Two things happened this round, and they're pretty different in size, so splitting them.

First, small one. Adding Crisp broke something I didn't expect: the whole page could
be dragged sideways, and only after doing that you'd actually see the chat icon.
Turns out overflow-x: hidden doesn't really mean "you can't scroll this direction" —
it just hides the scrollbar, the scrollable area is technically still there, and any
element sitting off-canvas (which is basically how Crisp's widget works before it's
positioned) can still be dragged into view on trackpad/touch. Switched to
overflow-x: clip instead, which actually removes the scrollable region rather than
just hiding it. Also hooked up Crisp's onChatOpened/onMessageSent events while I was
in there, since I was just calling .configure() and never listening for anything.

Second, the bigger one. I want the platform to feel goal-oriented not just to the
visitor, but to me — I don't want to be checking things manually forever, I want
something running in the background that tells me what's happening and eventually
handles the busywork itself.

Before building any "AI agent" thing, I needed the boring part first: actually
knowing what visitors do on the site. So:
- every page view gets logged
- every meaningful action gets logged too (contact form submit, audit run + completed,
  chat opened, chat message sent)
- all of it goes into Supabase through a serverless function, never straight from
  the browser — the browser only ever talks to my own /api/track endpoint

why not just use Crisp's or some analytics tool's own dashboard for this
- I want ONE place with MY definitions of what matters (audit run vs audit completed
  vs someone who just opened chat and left), not whatever a third party decides
  to show me
- this data needs to feed the reports AND the agent later, so it has to live
  somewhere I control the schema of

Once that existed, the natural next question was: where do I actually LOOK at this.
Options I thought through:
- a hidden route inside this same app, like /admin, behind a login
- a completely separate project, not linked from here at all

Went with fully separate. Reason is simple once you think about it: a "hidden" route
still ships its code to every visitor's browser as part of the JS bundle — hidden
just means I didn't put a link to it anywhere, not that it's actually invisible.
Anyone poking at devtools could find it. A separate project means none of that code,
and none of its secrets, are anywhere near what a visitor's browser ever downloads.

So started brightwave-ops. Own repo, own Vercel project, own Supabase project even
(so a leak in one doesn't touch the other). What's in it so far:
- login (just me, magic link, plus a server-side allowlist check so even a leaked
  login link to someone else can't actually see data)
- three report windows: morning (12am-12:30pm), evening (12:30pm-9pm),
  night (9pm-12am) — closed the gap that was in my original time split so the
  whole day actually adds up
- an approval queue — this is the "agent" part. It doesn't send anything on its
  own. It drafts (using Claude) and queues an action, I see it in the dashboard,
  I approve / edit-then-approve / reject. Nothing goes out without me looking at it
  first, same principle as the ads compliance stuff I already decided on

what's NOT built yet, on purpose
- nothing calls the drafting endpoint automatically yet — no cron job scanning
  for leads and proposing outreach
- nothing sends an approved action anywhere yet — approving just changes its
  status right now
- these are next, but wanted the foundation (tracking + reports + queue) solid
  and reviewed before wiring up anything that actually reaches out to a real person
